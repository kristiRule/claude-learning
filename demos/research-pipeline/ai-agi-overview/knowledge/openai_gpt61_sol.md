## Claims

- [fact] **GPT-6.1 Sol released 2026-09-29**, 26 days after GPT-6 Astra.
- [fact — IMPORTANT CORRECTION] **GPT-6.1 Sol does not supersede Astra.**
  It is positioned as the **mid-tier** reasoning model in the GPT-6 family,
  delivering performance *approaching* flagship Astra on agentic coding,
  computer use and professional workflows, at roughly **one-fifth of
  Astra's standard API token prices**. Astra remains OpenAI's flagship.
  A higher point number does not mean a newer flagship — a genuinely
  useful trap to show an audience learning to read this market.
- [fact] The Sol / Terra / Luna tier naming carried forward from GPT-5.6
  into the GPT-6 generation, confirming OpenAI's stated framing that the
  number is the generation and the name is a durable capability tier.
- [fact] Astra's own release notes report an updated Codex harness giving
  **1.9× faster task completion** than GPT-5.6 Sol on Mind2Web, prompt
  injection robustness up from **96.23% to 99.79%**, and a new mechanism
  that replaces repeated summarization with searchable notes when the
  context window fills.

## Limitations

- "One-fifth the price" and the Mind2Web and robustness figures are
  OpenAI's own. Attribute.
- This project previously mis-read GPT-6.1 as a successor flagship that
  made Astra stale. It is not. Corrected 2026-10-17 before anything
  reached a slide.

## Relevance

Two jobs. It prevents a factual error in the refresh — the deck's 15
references to GPT-6 Astra are still accurate, because Astra is still the
flagship.

And it is the second pillar of the **cost-collapse** thesis: within a
month of a flagship launch, the same lab shipped near-flagship capability
at a fifth of the price. Alongside Opus 5.5 at 40% cheaper and Vera Rubin
at 10× lower cost per token, the pattern for Q4 2026 is not that models
got much smarter — it is that the same capability got dramatically cheaper.
