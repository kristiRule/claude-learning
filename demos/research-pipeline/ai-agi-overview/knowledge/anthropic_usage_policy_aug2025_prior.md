## Claims

- [fact] **Published 2025-08-15, effective 2025-09-15.** The revision
  immediately prior to the one this run covers — roughly 14 months earlier.
  Anthropic's stated rationale then: clarity and detail based on user
  feedback, product changes, regulatory developments, and enforcement
  priorities — nearly identical framing to the 2026 update's own
  rationale.
- [fact] **Cybersecurity and agentic use** (new section at the time):
  prohibited malicious computer/network/infrastructure compromise.
  Rationale: agentic tools bring risks of "scaled abuse, malware creation,
  and cyber attacks." Vulnerability discovery with the system owner's
  consent remained supported.
- [fact] **Political content:** this is the revision that *first* imposed
  the blanket ban on lobbying and campaign content that the **2026 update
  then removed**. At the time, the broad ban was itself introduced to
  replace an even looser prior standard; Anthropic's 2025 framing was that
  the old approach limited legitimate policy research, civic education,
  and political writing — the **same stated justification reused almost
  verbatim in 2026** to justify removing the rule the 2025 update had just
  added.
- [fact] **Law enforcement language:** clarified wording; Anthropic stated
  this did not change what was allowed or prohibited. Surveillance,
  tracking, profiling, biometric monitoring remained restricted;
  back-office/analytical uses remained supported.
- [fact] **High-risk consumer-facing use cases:** requirements (human
  oversight, AI disclosure) for legal/financial/employment uses were
  scoped specifically to consumer-facing outputs, not B2B interactions.
  Rationale: enterprise use had expanded.

## Limitations

- This file documents the **superseded** version. Nothing here should be
  cited as the current policy — it exists in this run specifically as the
  baseline for year-over-year comparison.
- Retrieved via a URL (`anthropic.com/news/usage-policy-update`) that is
  **also the URL a careless search would return for the 2026 update** —
  see the run's README for the full note on this trap.

## Relevance

The through-line across the two revisions is genuinely interesting and
not obvious from either announcement read alone: **the 2025 update
tightened political-content rules, and the 2026 update loosened them
again, citing the same stated harm (chilling legitimate civic work) both
times** — once as the reason to restrict, once as the reason to relax.
That is either a sign of a policy still finding its footing on a hard
tradeoff, or a sign that the 2025 tightening overshot and Anthropic is
now saying so without quite saying so. Worth stating plainly in any
deliverable rather than smoothing over — it's the most concrete evidence
in this run that "policy clarification" sometimes means "we got the last
one wrong."
