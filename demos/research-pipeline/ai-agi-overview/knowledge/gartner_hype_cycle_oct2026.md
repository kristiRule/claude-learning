## Claims

- [fact] Positions hold from the April read: **agentic AI at the Peak of
  Inflated Expectations, generative AI in the Trough of Disillusionment.**
- [fact — NEW, AND THE CTO-FACING NUMBER] As of **2026-10-01** the stated
  concern has shifted from **model capability to application reliability
  and return on investment**. Organizations averaged **$1.9 million per
  generative AI initiative**, and **fewer than 30% of AI leaders report
  their CEO is satisfied with the return.**
- [fact] Gartner's framing of the trough: *"the problem is proof, not
  capability."* The models work; the business case is unproven.
- [fact] Agentic adoption: **17% of organizations have deployed AI agents**
  against **more than 60% expecting to within two years**. Gartner predicts
  **more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027**
  — a sharper figure than the deck's current "a good chunk."
- [fact] Structural change in the 2026 edition: **governance, security and
  cost management appear as standalone technology profiles**, not as
  footnotes to the agentic AI story.

## Limitations

- Gartner's full reports are paywalled; figures are corroborated across
  multiple secondary summaries rather than read from the primary document.
  Same limitation as `gartner_hype_cycle_2026`, which this supersedes for
  anything dated after April 2026.
- The "<30% of CEOs satisfied" figure is a survey of AI leaders reporting
  on their CEOs' satisfaction, not a survey of CEOs. Phrase carefully.

## Relevance

**Elevated priority: the user's CTO reads Gartner**, so this section is
load-bearing for a specific stakeholder rather than optional bonus
material, despite sitting after the close.

The ROI numbers are the upgrade. The deck's current Gartner slide explains
*where things sit on the curve*; it does not say *why*, and "why" is the
part a CTO is actually asking. $1.9M average spend against under 30% CEO
satisfaction is the single most quotable number in this source, and it
reframes the trough as a proof problem rather than a technology problem —
which is a more useful and less defeatist message than the curve alone.

Also strengthens the existing Responsible AI section: governance and cost
management being promoted to standalone profiles is independent
corroboration that this is where enterprise attention has moved.
