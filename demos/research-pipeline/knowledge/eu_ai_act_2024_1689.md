## Claims

- [fact] The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is a risk-based
  regulation that sorts AI systems into tiers by potential harm, with
  heavier obligations attached to higher-risk tiers.
- [fact] It applies to any AI system placed on the EU market or used
  within the EU, regardless of whether the provider is based outside
  the EU.
- [fact] As of August 2, 2026: main transparency duties (Article 50,
  covering chatbots, deepfakes, emotion-recognition, and AI-generated
  content disclosure) and general-purpose-AI (GPAI) provider obligations
  are in force, along with the EU AI Office's enforcement powers over
  GPAI providers. Prohibited-practice rules and AI-literacy duties are
  also unaffected by later delays.
- [fact] High-risk obligations were delayed via omnibus agreement: for
  stand-alone high-risk systems (Annex III — hiring, credit scoring,
  education, critical infrastructure) to December 2, 2027; for high-risk
  AI embedded in already-regulated products (Annex I — medical devices,
  machinery, toys) to August 2, 2028.
- [fact] Penalties for non-compliance reach up to €35M or 7% of global
  annual turnover, whichever is higher (lower cap for SMEs).

## Limitations

Extraction from search-result summaries of the regulation and its 2026
compliance-deadline amendments, not a direct read of the full legal text.
The delay dates in particular should be spot-checked against the EUR-Lex
consolidated text or an official EU Commission/AI Office statement before
being stated as fixed dates on a slide — omnibus/compliance timelines in
EU legislation have moved before and could move again.

Anthropic's voluntary self-governance (RSP). Good pairing: "here's a lab
policing itself, here's a government policing everyone."
